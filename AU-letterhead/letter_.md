The Promotion and Tenure Committee of the School of Computer and Cyber
Sciences has examined the record of Assistant Professor Dr. Gokila
Dorai, who has been on the tenure track at Augusta University since
August 2020. Dr. Dorai's workload allocation has emphasized research,
with 60% research, 30% teaching, and 10% service.

The Committee carefully evaluated Dr. Dorai's record of accomplishments
and determined that she has met the expectations of the tenure-track
position. After reviewing her contributions in research, teaching,
service, student success, and professional development, the Committee
unanimously recommends her for tenure and promotion.

# Teaching / Mentoring

Dr. Dorai teaching evaluations consistently highlight her strong
effectiveness as an instructor, with students praising her clarity,
organization, and ability to make complex material accessible. Across
multiple courses, including CSCI 1200 (Intro to Computers &
Programming), CYBR 4400 (Digital Forensics), and CSCI 6540 (Digital
Forensics and Machine Learning)---quantitative ratings are very
positive, with averages typically ranging between 4.1 and 5.0 out of 5 with frequently over 50% participation rate.
Students consistently note that she clearly conveys expectations, treats
students with respect, provides meaningful learning experiences, and
shows strong interest in student success.

Qualitative feedback reinforces these strengths: students frequently
describe her teaching as interactive, engaging, and supportive,
particularly appreciating her use of hands-on labs, projects, and
real-world applications such as digital forensics casework and industry
tools. Many noted her willingness to provide additional help, her
responsiveness to questions, and her use of innovative methods such as
flipped classrooms, peer instruction, and project-based learning.
Comments also emphasized how her classes connected theory to practice,
with activities like guest speakers from agencies such as the FBI and
field trips to GBI offices making learning especially impactful.

Peer evaluations echo these findings, describing her as professional,
polished, and effective at presenting material with clarity. She was
commended for her logical lesson structure, coherent explanations, and
ability to highlight key points. Overall, both student and peers acknowledge
a consistent pattern of teaching excellence,
with strong evidence of student engagement, innovative pedagogy, and
impactful mentorship.

# Research / Scholarship

Dr. Dorai research and scholarship record is both substantial and
impactful, demonstrating national and international recognition in the
areas of digital forensics, IoT security, and privacy-preserving
technologies. She has secured over \$1.2 million in competitive external
funding as a PI, including a \$750,000 NSF grant for socio-technical frameworks
in digital evidence handling, a \$495,000 NSA award for cyber-attack
detection, and additional support from the NSF-European Research Council
Collaboration Initiative. These achievements reflect her ability to lead
major research initiatives and attract high-profile funding in highly
competitive programs such as NSF. Her scholarship is distinguished by
two awarded U.S. patents (Content-hiding Software
Identification/Extraction System and Targeted Data Extraction System),
both of which represent innovative and practical solutions with direct
applications for law enforcement and industry.

Her publication record is equally strong, with more than 25
peer-reviewed articles presented at top venues such as IEEE ISDF, ARES,
SecureComm, ISNCC, and IFIP WG 11.9. She has received notable
recognition, including a Best Paper Award at the ARES-ICS-CSR Conference
in Vienna (2024). Her work is cited for its interdisciplinary
contributions that balance investigative needs with privacy concerns,
advancing knowledge in IoT intrusion detection, adversarial biometrics,
blockchain-based forensics, and mobile data extraction. Importantly, her
research has attracted attention from law enforcement agencies such as
Interpol, the Georgia Bureau of Investigation, and other state and
federal entities, reflecting the practical significance and broad
applicability of her work.

In addition to her individual scholarship, she has demonstrated strong
mentorship, supervising Ph.D. and undergraduate students who have
excelled in national competitions such as the DFRWS Forensics Challenge
and the NSA Codebreaker Challenge. Her students have won research awards
and achieved top national rankings, which underscores her effectiveness
in integrating students into her research program. Collectively, her
record of competitive funding, patents, publications, global
collaborations, and impactful mentorship clearly establishes her as a
leading scholar in cybersecurity and digital forensics, with a
trajectory of continued success and growing international reputation.

# Service

Dr. Dorai has an extensive record of leadership, community engagement,
and academic service at Augusta University and beyond. As Director of
Access, Success, and Belonging, she has spearheaded initiatives to
foster an inclusive academic environment, earning recognition through
ASB awards and leadership appointments. She has also contributed to
institutional discussions on AI integration, served as an AI panelist at
the Spring Academic Leadership Meeting, and evaluated faculty
development proposals for early-career awards.

Her community engagement leadership is evident in her service on the
Community Engagement Leadership Council (CELC), where she expanded
outreach to law enforcement by delivering training on AI applications
for police chiefs, and by presenting at the TechNet Conference organized
by AFCEA. In academic excellence, she served on the organizing committee
for the DFRWS USA 2025 Conference, contributed to AU's Faculty Research
Committee Collaborative Endeavor, and joined the Faculty Advisory
Committee for Undergraduate Research and Scholarship (CURS).

She has shown a strong commitment to student organizations and
mentorship, serving as lead faculty advisor for the Computer & Cyber
Graduate Student Organization (CCGSO)---recognized with a Distinguished
Service Award---and co-advising chapters such as WiCyS, UPE, and Girls
Who Code. She has mentored Cybercorps Scholarship For Service (SFS) Scholars, honors students, and Ph.D.
candidates, guiding them toward academic and professional success.

At the professional level, she has contributed as an NSF Panelist, NSF
Site Reviewer, conference chair, editor for the International Conference
on Microservices (2022), and reviewer for journals like *Forensic
Science International*. Her service has been recognized with honors
including keynote speaker invitations, the Emerging Scientist Award, and
the Golden Pen Award. Collectively, her service demonstrates sustained
leadership, impact, and recognition at institutional, community, and
international levels.

# Student Success Activities

Dr. Dori has deep commitment to student achievement through mentorship,
advising, and structured opportunities for academic and professional
growth. As Director of Access, Success, and Belonging at Augusta
University's School of Computer and Cyber Sciences, she has led
initiatives that support students across research, teaching, and
service.

She has served as a major advisor for four Ph.D. students (Bradley
Boswell, Seth Barrett, Rajon Bardhan, and Ashiqur Rahman), offering
consistent mentorship on publications, research methodologies, and
professional development. Her guidance has led to significant
accomplishments, such as mentoring Md. Ashiqur Rahman to a 5th-place
finish at the 2023 DFRWS Challenge on Industrial Control System
Forensics---with Augusta University being the only U.S. institution
ranked---and supporting his success in the 2024 NSA Codebreaker
Challenge, where he placed in the top 10% nationally. Additionally,
Rahman received *The Graduate School Award of Excellence in Research* at
Augusta University's 2025 Graduate Research Day Competition.

Her undergraduate mentoring has been equally impactful, involving
students like Logan VanPutte, Seth Barrett, Beatriz Rodriguez, and Ryan
Hawkins. Many of these students have contributed to co-authored
publications, professional conference presentations, and peer-reviewed
venues. These experiences not only honed their technical and research
skills but also positioned them competitively for advanced
opportunities.

Beyond structured advising, she has engaged students in collaborative
research projects and publications, helping them develop independence
while contributing meaningfully to the scholarly community. By fostering
excellence across diverse groups---Ph.D., undergraduate, SFS Scholars,
and honors students---she has built a strong record of mentoring that
integrates academic rigor with professional readiness, demonstrating her
sustained dedication to student success.

# Professional Growth and Development

Dr. Dorai spent and extensive efforts to strengthen her teaching,
research, and leadership capacities through certifications, trainings,
and academic development. She has actively pursued technical courses and
certifications that enhance her expertise in cybersecurity and digital
forensics, such as the Applied Data Science & AI/Machine Learning for
Cybersecurity Professionals certification from the SANS Institute
(2024), and professional certifications from the Information Assurance
Certification Review Board (IACRB), including Certified Computer
Forensics Examiner (CCFE) and Certified Mobile Forensics Examiner
(CMFE). She also completed the AX200 Magnet AXIOM Examination
certification, further strengthening her applied technical skills.

Her professional development extends to pedagogical training, including
participation in the Facilitating Entering Mentoring program through the
Center for the Improvement of Mentored Experiences in Research (CIMER)
at Augusta University (2025). This training focused on enhancing
mentorship, communication, and support for diverse students. She has
also contributed to advancing the field through her work as a reviewer
and member of professional organizations such as the American Academy of
Forensic Sciences (AAFS), reflecting her commitment to broadening
forensic science education and practice.

Furthermore, she has engaged in leadership and service-based
development, including participation in Augusta University's Spring
Academic Leadership Meeting and faculty development workshops. These
experiences have deepened her ability to integrate ethical
considerations, effective communication, and diversity awareness into
both her teaching and leadership roles.

Overall, her professional development trajectory demonstrates a
consistent investment in updating technical expertise, refining
pedagogy, and advancing leadership skills, all of which strengthen her
ability to contribute meaningfully to Augusta University and the broader
cybersecurity research and education community.
